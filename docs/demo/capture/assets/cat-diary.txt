MIA: A RECORD

Kept by her owner, who is fine, and who is writing this down so that future-me
knows it happened, in order, from the beginning.


2 March 2026

Six weeks with Mia. She is a Russian Blue, which the rescue told me twice, with a
grey-blue coat that changes shade when the light moves, and green eyes, and a way
of looking at a room that I can only describe as counting. The vet says she is
"remarkably calm", which I wrote down in a nice notebook because it felt like a
compliment at the time.

Tonight she sat on the arm of the sofa and looked at the top corner of the room
for forty minutes. Not at the wall. At the corner. I checked. No spider, no
insect, no draught. Nothing. Then she yawned, stretched, and walked off like she
had finished reading something. I watched her go and she did not look back.


9 March 2026

She walks on the keyboard. That is normal, I am told. What is less normal is
that she does it in the one second I am not looking, that she closes the file I
am working on, and that she then sits on the closed laptop with her front paws
tucked exactly as though she has never been anywhere near it. I opened it again.
The file was gone. I had not saved it. She watched me type the whole thing out a
second time. She did not blink once.


17 March 2026

The silence has a shape now. I can tell when she is in the room because the flat
gets quieter. She was behind the curtain and I knew before I looked. Her tongue
was still out from washing, mid-lick, completely stopped. She held it for a good
four seconds after I said her name, then put it away and left like I had
interrupted something.


24 March 2026

Mia pushed my mug off the table at 8:15. Not unusual. Cats knock things
over. What I want noted is that she did not watch it fall. Every other cat I have
met would have watched it fall. She looked at me, held it, then turned and walked
into the hallway. Like a man leaving after putting down a card.


1 April 2026

I bought a child lock for the treat cupboard. It is still on, still locked, and
this morning the cupboard was open. One treat was placed on the kitchen floor
in the exact centre of the room. No wrapper, no scattered food, no mess. One
treat, in the middle of the floor, like a calling card. She was already sitting
three feet away when I came downstairs. I said, "How." She blinked slowly. I have
read that slow blinking means trust. I am no longer sure what she is trusting me
with.

Later I looked up what it is called when someone leaves a single object in an
agreed place with nothing else around it. The internet says: a signal. The
internet also says: a dead drop marker. I closed the laptop, went and stood in
the kitchen and looked at the treat for a while, and then I ate it.


11 April 2026

Went under the bed for a phone charger and found forty-one socks. I have forty-
one socks I do not own and one I do. The one I own is the pair to the sock I
lost the week I moved in, which is three years ago, which is a year before I got
her. They were arranged in a spiral, biggest at the outside, smallest in the
middle. The middle sock was tiny and grey and I have never owned tiny grey socks.
I put everything back exactly how I found it. I do not want her to know I know.

I drew the spiral on paper afterwards, from memory, with the sizes right. From
above it is not a spiral. From above it is the plan of a flat with one room
crossed out, and I know it is the plan of a flat because it is my flat, and the
room crossed out is the room where I keep the ironing board and nothing else.


19 April 2026

She turned the bathroom tap on. Cats can do this, I have seen the videos. She
drank from it with her head tilted, then she reached up with one paw and turned
it off. All the way off, to the point where it does not drip, which is a thing I
cannot reliably do with my own hand. Then she washed her paw in the sink like she
was rinsing something off it. Then she left. I stood in the doorway for a while
and tried to build an innocent explanation and got most of the way there before
giving up.


2 May 2026

The bathroom door was ajar. The toilet lid was up. Mia was on the seat with
her front paws propped on the cistern and her back legs braced like a small
grey athlete. I want to be very clear: this was not a cat balanced on a rim
out of curiosity. This was a cat using a toilet. She saw me. She did not startle,
she did not fall in, she did not do the guilty scramble. She held my eye, kept her
balance, and finished. Then she stepped down, reached up, and pressed the flush
with the flat of her paw, and stood in front of it watching the water go round
with what I can only describe as professional interest. I closed the door for
her.


13 May 2026

I found my phone on the hall floor with the battery warm. It was unlocked. There
was one app open. The cart had one item in it: wild-caught tuna, twelve-pack. I
assumed I had done it. People do this. People walk around with their phone and
their thumb slips and they add tuna to a basket. Except I do not, and I checked,
and the delivery address was already selected, and the payment method was the
one card I use for her. I removed the item and put the phone face down on the
counter and she sat next to it for the rest of the evening, not looking at it,
which is worse than looking at it.

In the morning I went back into the app and checked the search history, because
I am the sort of person who checks now. There was one earlier search. It was in
Cyrillic. I put it through a translator and it came back as "tuna", which is not
surprising, and what is surprising is the spelling of it, which is correct, in a
language I do not speak. I have five keyboard languages switched on. I added them
once by accident and never took them off. That is the explanation. I have written
it down so that I stop thinking about the other one.


20 May 2026

Ninety-six cans of tuna arrived. I had a two-factor code on the account by then,
which means the order went through in the window between me removing one can and
me adding the code. She was in the room for that window. She watched me set it up.
She sat beside me while I read the instructions out loud to myself. When I
finished, she reached over and tapped the screen once, gently, on the part where
it said "confirm".

I am now the owner of ninety-six cans of tuna and a very calm cat and a growing
suspicion that I am the one being trained.

Ninety-six cans. I did the maths, because that is what I do now instead of
sleeping. For one cat, that is four months of food. For a small group of people
who do not want to be seen going to the shops, that is a month of rations. I am
not saying which one it is. I am saying I did the maths.


1 June 2026

Three twelve in the morning. I woke up because somebody was talking.

Not a shout. Not a noise in another room. A voice, close to my ear, at the
volume of a person being considerate of a sleeper, and patient, and calm, and it
said:

"You should buy a second cat bed."

I opened my eyes. Mia was on the pillow by my head, eyes closed, purring.
Purring is not talking. Purring is a courting and contentment sound, it is
documented, it is a vibration in a throat and not language. I know that. I lay
there in the dark and told myself that, several times, out loud, and she purred
louder, which is what a cat does when you speak to it, which is fine.


4 June 2026

I bought a second cat bed. I do not need a second cat bed and I did not want a
second cat bed. I have one cat and one bed and she sleeps on the laundry. It
arrived on Thursday and I set it up in the corner of the living room and stood
back and felt strange about the whole thing. She slept in the cardboard box it
came in for nine hours. The bed is still empty. I am leaving it there.


15 June 2026

I tripped over a length of yarn stretched across the hallway at ankle height.
Tied to the door handle, pulled tight, wrapped twice around the skirting board
on the other side. I know how it got there, obviously. I did not put it there. She
was sitting in the doorway with her tail going, watching me untangle my foot,
and when I got free she did the little chirp. That chirp is for birds. She uses it
on me now. She uses it when the trap works.


16 June 2026

Today's was better. Cling film across the doorway between the kitchen and the
hall, held up with tape pressed flat, at exactly my face height, and I walked
into it in the dark carrying the recycling. I shouted. She was inside the kitchen,
behind me, so that I set it off from the wrong side, which means she planned for
which way I would come in. She made a sound I have not heard from her before. I am
calling it a laugh because I do not have better equipment than that.


29 June 2026

My keys were in her food bowl. I only knew because she was sitting beside the bowl
staring at me. Two days later they were in the freezer, behind the peas. Then in
the toe of a boot by the door. Every time, she was already in position, waiting,
before I started looking. She clearly enjoys the search itself. Twice I gave up
and sat down and she got up and led me to them and sat down again with the air of
someone who has won a bet.


8 July 2026

She watches television. Not near it, not the moving colours of it - she sits
facing the screen, at a sensible distance, and watches. There was a documentary
on wading birds. She watched the entire thing with her ears going in different
directions. When it finished, she went to the window, sat on the sill and looked
out at the garden for two hours in the dark, not moving. Then she came back,
walked across the remote, and the channel changed back to the same programme
which was now repeating. She stayed for the repeat.

I have decided not to read anything into this.


12 July 2026

At three this morning the neighbour's cat arrived on the outside of the kitchen
window. The big black one from number 9, the one that is not allowed out. Mia was
already on the sill, facing it, and they looked at each other for eight minutes
without a sound. No hissing. No ears back. No slow blink, which is what cats do
when they are trying to be polite. Then the black cat left, and Mia came upstairs
and went to sleep.

I have seen two cats meet at a window before. They shout. These two did not
shout. I am going to stop calling it a meeting and start calling it what it
looked like, which is a briefing.


20 July 2026

My phone rang in the kitchen while I was in the garden. I got to it late: one
missed call from Mum. She said she had not been able to hear me, and then she
said something I have been carrying around all week: that she had heard
breathing, and then a purr, and then a small click as the call ended. She said,
"Your cat was very polite on the phone."

I checked the call length. Four minutes eleven seconds. She was sitting on the
phone, on the kitchen counter, when I came in. She had moved it off the charging
cable first, which is the part that keeps me up.


26 July 2026

Work call, camera on, seven people. Mia came in, walked the length of the
desk in front of the lens, turned round, and sat down facing the camera with her
paws together. Nobody said anything for a moment and then three people said she
looked like she was chairing the meeting. My manager asked if she had opinions on
the roadmap. She blinked. I said, out of habit, "She's just saying hello," and then
realised I had answered for her, quickly, like a man covering.


5 August 2026

There was kibble on the kitchen floor when I came down. Five pieces. One above,
two across, two below. I want to be careful about this. It could have been a
cross. It could have been a plus sign. It could have been gravity and a bag and a
cat doing nothing at all. It could have been a "t".


9 August 2026

Eighteen pieces of kibble this morning, laid out in a line, in four groups, and
the groups were letters, and the letters said MORE. I got down on the floor with
my cheek on the tiles to check it from every angle. It says MORE. She came and sat
in front of it, facing me, and watched me read it, and when I said "no" she turned
around and knocked it flat with her tail and walked out.

I have not slept properly since.


18 August 2026

The courier rang. By the time I got to the door the parcel was on the step and
the courier was laughing and Mia was sitting on the mat. The courier said,
"Your cat let me know." It turns out that the wheelie bin had been pushed round
so the front door could not swing shut, which means the door was standing open
when the courier arrived, which means the house was open to the street for the
four minutes it took me to walk downstairs. Nothing was taken. The parcel had
been moved inside. To the hall table. Which is where I put things.

The courier asked if she was allowed treats. Mia sat up. She is not allowed
treats. Everyone keeps asking her.


21 August 2026

I said it out loud tonight, as a joke, with the kettle on and my back to her,
which is how I say most things I do not want to commit to. I said, "You know,
some people would say you are a KGB operative."

She was washing. She finished the paw she was on. She put it down. She did not
look up. She did not startle, and she did not do the small ear-turn that cats do
when you say their name. She got off the chair, walked out of the kitchen, came
back four minutes later, and sat with her back to me for twenty minutes.

She does that when I call her the wrong name. I have witnesses. She turns her
back and sits down and stays there, and everybody who has met her has seen her do
it. Which means she heard the first thing. Which means she has heard it before.


30 August 2026

Vet visit. General check, no concerns, weight good, teeth good, heart good.
Deeply healthy. The vet said, "Is she always like this?" and I said, "Like
what?" and she said, "She hasn't moved. She's been holding still since I got the
thermometer out."

She took her own temperature. With her eyes open, looking at me, while a stranger
put a thing in her that every other animal in that building had to be held down
for. When she finished she got up, sat down neatly, and waited to be told she had
done well.

There is one more thing. On the way out, she looked at the other cat in the
waiting room. It was small and it went flat against its carrier. It made itself
smaller. She did nothing. She just looked, and then walked out with me, and that
cat's owner said, "Yours is very well behaved." Well behaved. That is the phrase
she used.

At the desk I asked her to scan the chip. I wanted the number on paper, because I
have stopped trusting paper. The number matches the paperwork. The rescue record
lists her as found, no history, and as a name which is four letters and a number,
and the vet said that is completely normal for a rescue.

She said it twice. I noticed the second one. Then she looked at me the way people
look at someone who is about to ask a third question, so I did not ask the third
question.


7 September 2026

The front door lock has a keypad. The code has been changed. I did not change
it. The new code works, and it is written on the fridge in magnets: four numbers,
which are the day and month of my birthday, with the second digit missing. I
have never told anyone the code. I have typed it in front of her, many times,
looking over my shoulder at her the last few weeks, which now feels like the
actual method.


9 September 2026

I put a small bell on her collar on Friday. I told myself it was so I would know
where she was. The real reason was that I wanted to hear her coming, and I am
writing that down because I have started to notice how many of my decisions
lately are made for that reason.

This morning the collar was on the kitchen floor, unbuckled, and the bell was at
the bottom of her water bowl, under the surface, put there on purpose. She has
never taken her collar off. She has never shown the slightest interest in it.

The bell is still in the bowl. I have not taken it out. I think it is a reply, and
I think the reply is: I heard you too.


12 September 2026

She sits at the front door at ten to eleven every morning. I thought she liked
the hallway. The post comes at eleven.

So I changed the post, for three days, to five in the afternoon, which is the
sort of thing you do when you have started testing a cat and do not want to say
so out loud. She sat at the door at ten to five. Three days running. Ten minutes
before, every time, and she did not check the window first and she did not go and
smell the door. She was simply already correct.

I have started calling it the schedule, out loud, in front of her, in the voice
you use when you are joking. She has started looking at me when I say it. That is
the part that is not funny.


15 September 2026

The laser pointer has gone missing. I found it under the sofa cushion where I
sit, pointing at the treat tin. She was on the floor, two feet from it, waiting.
I picked it up. I clicked it. I gave her a treat. I only worked out afterwards
what had just happened: the pointer was aimed, it was in my seat, I did the
clicking and the giving, and she did the sitting there.

It is not a clever trap. It is a cheap trap. And she is not supposed to know the
sound the treat tin makes, and she does, and she knew it would be me that made it.


24 September 2026

Woke up to the coffee machine running. Mug was on the counter in the right
place, under the spout, dry. Mia was sitting on the counter next to it,
awake, facing the front of the machine, absolutely straight-backed. There was a
second mug. Small. Filled with water. She watched me look at it, then looked at
the small mug, then looked at me.

I drank the coffee. It was fine. It was better than mine.


2 October 2026

A parcel came addressed to her. I am not making this up and I have the label.
"How to Train Your Human: A Cat's Guide", paperback, ordered on my account, one
click, no 2FA prompt, which means it came from a device that was already signed
in.

She has started it. There is a bookmark at page thirty-four.

The bookmark is my bank card.


9 October 2026

Page ninety-one now. I know because I checked while she was asleep, which felt
like snooping in someone's diary, which I want to point out is a joke I am
making because it is not funny, because I am the one with the diary.

Highlights so far, from what I can see in the margins: "humans respond well to
silence". "Establish the sound before you need it". "A closed door is a
suggestion".

There is a chapter near the back called "Cover". The corner of that page has been
folded over. I have never folded a page in my life. I use the bank card.

The margins are not the shocking part. The shocking part is that some of the
passages are underlined in pencil. And I do not own a pencil.


14 October 2026

Last night she was asleep on the book with one paw holding her place. Purring.
Just a cat, on a paperback, in a warm room, and I stood in the doorway for a
while and let myself believe that, all of it, the whole year, and it was a very
comfortable thing to believe.

Then I went to shut the door.

She did not lift her head. She did not open her eyes. She said, quietly, into the
page, in a voice that was not a purr and was not a chirp and was not a sound I
have ever heard a body that size make:

"Leave it."

I left it. I am leaving it. I am writing this down in the morning and then I am
going to buy her a second bed.


16 October 2026

I have stopped testing her. Every test comes back the same way and the result is
not the point any more. But I wanted to know whose cat she was, and there is only
one way to ask a cat that, so I got the paperwork out of the drawer. The rescue
number, the four letters, the number, and the date she was found, which is three
days before I was told she had arrived.

I put it all back. Then I shut the drawer.

She sat on the drawer. Not the laptop, not the sofa, not the radiator. The
drawer, from four in the afternoon until I went to bed, facing the room, with her
tail wrapped round her feet.

Mum rang to ask how Mia was. I said she was fine, and then I heard myself say,
without having decided to, "She's very good at her job."

Mum was quiet. My mother has never been quiet in her life. Then she said, "Oh,
that's nice, dear," in a voice I have never heard her use, asked about my
brother, and got off the phone quite quickly, and I stood in the hall holding it
for a while afterwards.

I have decided to stop writing this down. Not because I am frightened. Because I
have started to notice how easy this diary has been to keep. Every week,
something to write. All year, without a gap. As though somebody has been
arranging for me to have material.
